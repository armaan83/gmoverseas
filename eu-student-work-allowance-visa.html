EU Student Work Allowance 2025: How Many Hours Can You Work in Each Country?

Studying in Europe offers world-class education, but managing living expenses can be challenging. Many international students rely on part-time work to support themselves while gaining valuable experience. Fortunately, most EU countries allow foreign students to work legally during their studies, though the permitted hours and conditions vary. This guide breaks down the work allowance rules for students in each EU member state, helping you plan your finances and stay compliant with visa regulations.

General EU Rules for Student Work

While each country sets its own specifics, a common framework exists across the EU:

  • During semester/term: Typically up to 20 hours per week.
  • During holidays/vacations: Full-time work (usually 38-40 hours per week) is often permitted.
  • The work permission is usually tied to your student residence permit; no separate work visa is needed.
  • You must be enrolled in a recognized higher education institution.
  • Some countries require that the job does not interfere with your studies.

How to Ensure Compliance

  1. Check your residence permit: It should mention work rights or include a note about permitted employment.
  2. Keep track of your hours: Maintain a simple log to avoid exceeding limits.
  3. Prioritize studies: Ensure work does not negatively impact your academic performance.
  4. Seek on-campus jobs: Many universities offer positions (e.g., research assistant, library) that are automatically compliant.
  5. Consult your international student office: They can provide country-specific guidance.

Post-Study Work Opportunities

Many EU countries offer post-study work visas or residence permits that allow you to stay and work after graduation. Examples include:

  • Germany: 18-month residence permit for job seeking.
  • France: Temporary residence permit (APS) for up to 12 months.
  • Netherlands: Orientation year (zoekjaar) for up to 12 months.
  • Ireland: Stay Back Option for up to 24 months.
  • Sweden: Job seeker permit for up to 6 months.

These pathways can turn your study experience into a long-term career in Europe.

Key Takeaway

While the 20-hour/week rule during term is a useful benchmark, always verify the specific regulations for your chosen country and institution. Planning your work hours early helps you balance earnings, experience, and academic success.
