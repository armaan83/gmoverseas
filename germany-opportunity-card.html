Germany Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) 2025: Points, Salary & How Indians Can Apply Without a Job Offer

📅 Published: July 18, 2026 ⏱ Read Time: 9 mins

What if you could fly to Germany before you had a job lined up — legally — and spend up to a year interviewing, networking, and landing a role in one of the world's strongest economies? That is exactly what the Germany Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) makes possible for skilled Indians. Launched in June 2023 under Germany's Skilled Workers Immigration Act, it is the single biggest new lever for Indians who want a European foothold without a pre-arranged offer.

In this guide, we break down the exact points system, the 2025 salary floors, who qualifies, and the step-by-step path to apply from India — in plain language, with figures you can act on.

What Is the Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card)?

The Opportunity Card is a points-based job-seeker visa. Unlike a traditional work permit, you do not need a German employer's contract before you travel. Instead, you prove you are a qualified skilled professional, arrive in Germany on the card, and look for work on the ground.

While job-hunting, holders may work part-time (up to 20 hours per week) and take two trial jobs of up to two weeks each — a huge advantage over older job-seeker routes that banned employment entirely.

Why It Matters for Indians

Germany faces a chronic skilled-labour shortage and has signed an active Germany–India skilled-migration / mobility partnership. The Opportunity Card is the flagship route built for precisely this — Indian graduates and professionals can enter first, then convert to a work permit once hired.

The Points System: How You Qualify

You need at least 6 points from the categories below. The more boxes you tick, the stronger your application:

  • Recognised qualification (4 pts): A degree or vocational qualification recognised in Germany. Indian degrees often need a recognition check via anabin or ZAB.
  • Relevant work experience (3 pts): At least 3 years of experience in your field within the last 7 years.
  • Language skills (1–3 pts): German A2 = 1 pt, B1 = 2 pts, B2/C1 = 3 pts. English C1 (e.g. IELTS) can also contribute.
  • Prior Germany stay (1 pt): You have previously lived, studied, or worked in Germany.
  • Spouse qualification (1 pt): Your accompanying spouse also holds a recognised qualification.
  • Age (1–2 pts): Under 35 = 2 pts; 35–39 = 1 pt.

Salary Floor You Must Meet (2025 Figures)

Beyond points, you must show you can support yourself. The card sets a gross annual salary reference figure:

  • Standard track: approx. €43,800 per year
  • Shortage-occupation track (IT, engineering, healthcare, STEM): approx. €41,042 per year

You demonstrate this either through a concrete job offer at/above the threshold, or through proof of sufficient financial means (a blocked account, similar to the student route) to cover your stay while you search.

Blocked Account Reminder

As with the student visa, you will typically need a German blocked account (Sperrkonto) proving living funds for your job-seeking period. Plan this early — it is a mandatory financial proof, not optional.

Who Is It For? (Eligibility at a Glance)

  • Indian nationals with a recognised university degree or a recognised vocational qualification
  • At least 2 years of relevant professional experience (for the vocational route)
  • Basic German (A1) or English C1 as a minimum language base
  • Ability to fund the job-seeking stay (blocked account or equivalent)
  • No prior deportation or security concerns

Step-by-Step: How Indians Apply from India

  1. Check qualification recognition: Verify your degree on anabin / apply for a ZAB statement of comparability.
  2. Tally your points: Use the official points calculator to confirm you reach 6+.
  3. Open a blocked account: Deposit the required living funds with an approved provider (Expatrio, Fintiba, Coracle).
  4. Book a VFS appointment: Submit your Opportunity Card application at the German mission / VFS in India.
  5. Gather documents: Passport, qualification proof, experience letters, language cert, blocked-account confirmation, health insurance, cover letter.
  6. Travel & job-hunt: On approval, enter Germany and begin interviewing — legally working part-time while you search.
  7. Convert to a work permit: Once hired above the salary threshold, switch from the Opportunity Card to a residence permit for employment.

Opportunity Card vs EU Blue Card

Many applicants confuse the two. The simple distinction:

  • Opportunity Card: enter without a job; search on the ground; part-time work allowed.
  • EU Blue Card: requires a concrete job offer at ≥1.2× the German average salary; it is the premium work-permit track once you are hired.

In practice, Indians often use the Opportunity Card to arrive, then upgrade to the EU Blue Card after securing employment.
